\begin{abstract}
When an F-16 loses elevator authority, an erroneous control-effectiveness estimate in the adaptive loop can destabilize the vehicle faster than passive, unadapted flight. This paper presents the Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl) framework, formulating the reconfigurable control problem through the lens of analytical estimator certification. Using the Small-Gain Theorem, we establish an analytical sufficient stability condition bounding real-time estimation error in the linearized short-period loop ($-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$), defining an asymmetric design-point lower bound of $\tilde{\gamma}_e > \SmallGainBoundNominal$ ($\hat{\gamma}_e > \SmallGainMinGammaHat$) at $50\%$ elevator loss ($\gamma_e = 0.50$) and motivating an adaptive safety floor of $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = \SafetyFloorVal$. Across $n = 1600$ held-out test windows, a split-conformal verification layer establishes a distribution-free $\ge \ConformalCoverage$ coverage guarantee ($q_{\alpha=0.01} = \ConformalQuantile$), certifying that worst-case underestimation preserves a $\ConformalMarginDesignPoint$ safety margin to the Small-Gain boundary at the design point ($\ConformalMarginSafetyFloor$ at the safety floor, with sample margins $>3.7\times$ globally). Crucially, an out-of-scope propulsion boundary test demonstrates that 8-channel sequence estimation isolates unmodeled throttle ceiling faults ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = \TvQFullStackThrottle$), whereas single-channel adaptation misattributes airspeed decay as elevator loss, driving pitch excitation to $\text{TV}_q = \TvQMisattrThrottle$ ($\TvQThrottleSpikePct$). Closed-loop audits confirm that classical MMAE and gain-ratio detectors suffer severe calibration collapse under low trim excitation ($100\%$ false alarms, MAE $0.48$), whereas the sequence estimator retains uniform accuracy ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$) with $0\%$ false alarms under matched fairness protocols. All software, pre-trained weights, and simulation telemetry are open-sourced under the MIT License at \url{https://github.com/Sm-bello/PHI-CTRL} and permanently archived on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22767079).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Modern high-performance combat aircraft and autonomous atmospheric flight vehicles operate across demanding flight envelopes where mechanical actuator jamming, hydraulic pressure depletion, control-surface loss, and propulsion impairments pose critical loss-of-control hazards \cite{ZhangJiang2008,Edwards2010,Blanke2006}. To preserve survivability, Fault-Tolerant Flight Control Systems (FTFCS) must estimate remaining control-surface effectiveness and reconfigure feedback control laws in real time \cite{Steinberg2005,Bodson2002,Durham1993,Oppenheimer2006}.

Adaptive flight control architectures have evolved from gain-scheduled proportional-integral-derivative (PID) schemes toward Model Reference Adaptive Control (MRAC) \cite{Lavretsky2013,Ioannou1996}, $\mathcal{L}_1$ adaptive control \cite{Hovakimyan2010}, and neural-network-augmented architectures evaluated in flight test programs such as the NASA Intelligent Flight Control System (IFCS) on the NF-15B \cite{Bosworth2006,WilliamsHayes2005} and damage-adaptive flight control programs \cite{Nguyen2008}. Complementing model-based adaptive control, Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI) bypasses detailed aerodynamic modeling by relying on high-rate angular acceleration feedback \cite{StevensLewis2003}; however, INDI is inherently sensitive to sensor noise and transport delays, and it does not yield explicit parametric damage identification needed for mission replanning and energy management. Concurrently, total energy principles pioneered by Lambregts \cite{Lambregts1983,Voth1992,Bruce1986} established the Total Energy Control System (TECS) paradigm, coordinating altitude and airspeed tracking through specific total energy rate and energy distribution channels.

In learning-augmented reconfigurable flight control, feedforward control-effectiveness compensation via $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e) = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$ is textbook. However, this raises a foundational certification challenge: when deep neural networks, sequence models \cite{Hochreiter1997,Graves2013}, or digital twins \cite{Glaessgen2012,Tao2019} are embedded directly inside an adaptive flight-control loop, an erroneous estimate can destabilize the vehicle faster than passive, unadapted flight. Certification-oriented design requires bounding the permissible real-time estimation error $\tilde{\gamma}_e = \hat{\gamma}_e - \gamma_e$ against an analytical stability margin, backed by finite-sample statistical guarantees \cite{Vovk2005,Tibshirani2019,Angelopoulos2023}.

To resolve this challenge, this paper presents the \textbf{Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl)} framework, evaluated on a nonlinear 6-DOF JSBSim F-16 aircraft model. This study is framed not as a novel control-law contribution, but as an **estimator certification methodology**, using feedforward gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\max(\hat{\gamma}_e, \gamma_{\text{floor}})$ as the baseline law that makes the analytical stability question well-posed. The core contributions of this study are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Sufficient Stability Condition for Estimation Error (C1):} We derive a closed-form sufficient stability condition via the Small-Gain Theorem for multiplicative elevator effectiveness estimation errors $\tilde{\gamma}_e$ in the linearized scalar short-period pitch-rate loop. We prove that asymptotic stability of the linearized loop is guaranteed if $-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$, explaining mathematically why underestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e < 0$) is significantly more hazardous than overestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e > 0$), and providing an analytical derivation for an adaptive gain safety floor $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$.
    
    \item \textbf{Conformal Estimator Certification Methodology (C2):} We introduce a distribution-free split-conformal certification layer on an 8-channel multivariate CNN-BiLSTM sequence estimator (PHI-Twin). Across $n = 1600$ held-out test windows evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$ ($\ge 99\%$ confidence), the conformal underestimation bound $q_{\alpha=0.01} = 0.0477$ certifies a $4.59\times$ safety margin to the Small-Gain instability boundary at the $\gamma_e = 0.50$ design point and $3.21\times$ at the $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ safety floor, with empirical test coverage $\ge 99.0\%$ globally.
    
    \item \textbf{Input-Channel Decoupling vs.\ Out-of-Scope Fault Misattribution (C3):} By retraining architecturally identical 1-channel ($[q]$), 3-channel ($[q, \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}, \delta_{e,\text{meas}}]$), and 8-channel CNN-BiLSTMs under a matched fairness protocol, we demonstrate that input-channel observability—not model capacity—governs cross-channel fault isolation. When exposed to an unmodeled throttle ceiling fault ($\delta_{\text{th,plant}} = \gamma_{\text{th}} \times 0.50$), 8-channel sequence estimation isolates the fault ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = 457.5^\circ$), whereas single-channel adaptation misattributes airspeed decay as elevator loss, driving pitch excitation to $\text{TV}_q = 880.7^\circ$ ($+92.5\%$).
    
    \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Detector Calibration Contrast (C4):} We execute a detector calibration audit comparing classical lightweight FDI schemes (MMAE hypothesis banks, rolling GainRatio, RLS) against sequence estimation across 160 closed-loop telemetry episodes subjected to pitch-rate gyro bias $b_q \in [0.0, 5.0]\text{ deg/s}$. We show that feedback-induced trim excitation starvation ($\sigma(\delta_{e,\text{cmd}}) \approx 0.008$) causes discrete MMAE posteriors to collapse ($100\%$ healthy false alarms) and GainRatio to suffer bias swamping ($\text{MAE} \approx 0.48$), whereas sequence estimation retains calibration ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$, $0\%$ false alarms).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Nonlinear Flight Dynamics and State-Space Formulation}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{Six-Degree-of-Freedom Equations of Motion}
In body-fixed coordinates $F_b = \{O_b, X_b, Y_b, Z_b\}$ centered at the aircraft center of mass, the rigid-body six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) translational equations of motion \cite{StevensLewis2003} are:
\begin{align}
    \dot{u} &= rv - qw - g \sin\theta + \frac{X_{\text{aero}} + T \cos\phi_T}{m} \label{eq:u_dot} \\
    \dot{v} &= pw - ru + g \cos\theta \sin\phi + \frac{Y_{\text{aero}}}{m} \label{eq:v_dot} \\
    \dot{w} &= qu - pv + g \cos\theta \cos\phi + \frac{Z_{\text{aero}} - T \sin\phi_T}{m} \label{eq:w_dot}
\end{align}
where $[u, v, w]^T$ is the body-axis velocity vector, $m$ is aircraft mass, $g$ is gravitational acceleration, $[\phi, \theta, \psi]^T$ are Euler roll, pitch, and yaw angles, $T$ is net propulsive thrust at cant angle $\phi_T$, and $[X_{\text{aero}}, Y_{\text{aero}}, Z_{\text{aero}}]^T$ are aerodynamic body forces.

Assuming lateral-directional mass symmetry ($I_{xy} = I_{yz} = 0$), the rotational equations of motion for body angular rates $[p, q, r]^T$ with inertia tensor $\bm{I}$ are:
\begin{align}
    I_{xx} \dot{p} - I_{xz} \dot{r} &= (I_{yy} - I_{zz}) qr + I_{xz} pq + \mathcal{L}_{\text{aero}} \label{eq:p_dot} \\
    I_{yy} \dot{q} &= (I_{zz} - I_{xx}) pr - I_{xz} (p^2 - r^2) + \mathcal{M}_{\text{aero}} + z_T T \label{eq:q_dot} \\
    I_{zz} \dot{r} - I_{xz} \dot{p} &= (I_{xx} - I_{yy}) pq - I_{xz} qr + \mathcal{N}_{\text{aero}} \label{eq:r_dot}
\end{align}
where $[\mathcal{L}_{\text{aero}}, \mathcal{M}_{\text{aero}}, \mathcal{N}_{\text{aero}}]^T$ are aerodynamic rolling, pitching, and yawing moments, and $z_T$ is the vertical thrust moment arm relative to the center of gravity. Euler kinematics and airdata quantities follow standard definitions:
\begin{align}
    \dot\phi &= p + (q \sin\phi + r \cos\phi) \tan\theta, \quad \dot{\theta} = q \cos\phi - r \sin\phi \\
    V &= \sqrt{u^2 + v^2 + w^2}, \quad \alpha = \arctan\left(\frac{w}{u}\right), \quad \beta = \arcsin\left(\frac{v}{V}\right)
\end{align}

\subsection{Aerodynamic Expansions and Short-Period Linearization}
Longitudinal aerodynamic forces and pitching moment are parameterized by dynamic pressure $\bar{q} = \frac{1}{2} \rho V^2$, wing reference area $S$, and mean aerodynamic chord $\bar{c}$:
\begin{align}
    X_{\text{aero}} &= \bar{q} S \left( -C_D \cos\alpha + C_L \sin\alpha \right), \quad
    Z_{\text{aero}} = \bar{q} S \left( -C_D \sin\alpha - C_L \cos\alpha \right) \\
    \mathcal{M}_{\text{aero}} &= \bar{q} S \bar{c} \, C_m \\
    C_L &= C_{L_0} + C_{L_\alpha} \alpha + C_{L_q} \frac{\bar{c}}{2V} q + C_{L_{\delta_e}} \delta_e, \quad
    C_D = C_{D_0} + K C_L^2 \\
    C_m &= C_{m_0} + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha + C_{m_q} \frac{\bar{c}}{2V} q + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e
\end{align}
where $\delta_e$ is elevator deflection, $C_{m_{\delta_e}} < 0$ is elevator pitch effectiveness, and $C_{m_\alpha}$ characterizes longitudinal static stability (yielding dimensional derivative $M_\alpha = -0.2225\text{ s}^{-2}$ at the trimmed cruise condition, reflecting a statically stable short-period mode).

Linearizing the longitudinal flight dynamics about the level-flight cruise equilibrium ($h_0 = 15{,}000\text{ ft}$, $V_{\text{TAS},0} = 392.1\text{ kt} = 661.7\text{ ft/s}$, $V_{c,0} = 317.3\text{ kt}$, $\text{Mach } 0.6258$, $\bar{q} = 15.68\text{ kPa}$, $\alpha_0 = 1.659^\circ$, $\theta_0 = 1.659^\circ$, $\delta_{e,0} = -0.0453$ ($-1.13^\circ$), $\delta_{\text{th},0} = 0.2819$, $F_{T,0} = 2861.6\text{ lbf}$) with short-period state vector $\bm{x}_{sp} = [\Delta \alpha, \Delta q]^T$ and control input vector $\bm{u} = [\Delta \delta_e, \Delta \delta_{\text{th}}]^T$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{bmatrix} \dot{\alpha} \\ \dot{q} \end{bmatrix} =
    \begin{bmatrix} \frac{Z_\alpha}{V_0} & \frac{Z_q}{V_0} \\ M_\alpha & M_q \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} \alpha \\ q \end{bmatrix} +
    \begin{bmatrix} \frac{Z_{\delta_e}}{V_0} & \frac{Z_{\delta_{\text{th}}}}{V_0} \\ M_{\delta_e} & M_{\delta_{\text{th}}} \end{bmatrix}
    \begin{bmatrix} \delta_e \\ \delta_{\text{th}} \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eq:short_period}
\end{equation}
Numerical perturbation of the 6-DOF JSBSim F-16 plant at trim produces the dimensional stability derivatives:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{Z_\alpha}{V_0} = -0.8385\text{ s}^{-1}, \quad \frac{Z_q}{V_0} = 0.9237, \quad M_\alpha = -0.2225\text{ s}^{-2}, \quad M_q = -0.9586\text{ s}^{-1}
\end{equation}
and control effectiveness derivatives:
\begin{equation}
    M_{\delta_e} = -3.5577\text{ rad/s}^2, \quad \frac{Z_{\delta_e}}{V_0} = -0.8112\text{ s}^{-1}, \quad M_{\delta_{\text{th}}} = -0.1067\text{ rad/s}^2
\end{equation}
The characteristic determinant of the short-period system matrix satisfies:
\begin{align}
    \det(\bm{A}_{sp}) &= \frac{Z_\alpha}{V_0}\, M_q - M_\alpha\, \frac{Z_q}{V_0} \nonumber \\
    &= (-0.8385)(-0.9586) - (-0.2225)(0.9237) = +1.0093 > 0
\end{align}
yielding open-loop eigenvalues $\lambda_{1,2} = -0.8986 \pm 0.4493j\text{ rad/s}$ with damping ratio $\zeta_{sp} = 0.895$ and undamped natural frequency $\omega_{n,sp} = 1.005\text{ rad/s}$, confirming that at this cruise equilibrium condition, the short-period mode is dynamically stable.

Crucially, the ratio of control moment derivatives satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \frac{|M_{\delta_{\text{th}}}|}{|M_{\delta_e}|} = \frac{0.1067}{3.5577} = 0.0300
    \label{eq:coupling_ratio}
\end{equation}
demonstrating that the direct pitching moment generated by throttle actuation is $33.3\times$ smaller than elevator authority. Hence, pitch-rate perturbations observed under propulsion loss do not stem from direct thrust pitching moments, but rather from kinematic airspeed deceleration and energy redistribution.

\subsection{Effector Degradation Models}
Actuator degradation in the primary elevator campaigns is modeled as aerodynamic control effectiveness loss $\gamma_e(t) \in [0.25, 1.0]$:
\begin{equation}
    C_{m_{\delta_e,\text{eff}}}(t) = \gamma_e(t) C_{m_{\delta_e}}, \quad \mathcal{M}_{\text{aero},\delta_e}(t) = \bar{q} S \bar{c} \, \gamma_e(t) C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e(t)
    \label{eq:gamma_elev}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_e = 1.0$ corresponds to a healthy surface and $\gamma_e = 0.50$ represents a $50\%$ loss of aerodynamic pitch authority.

Crucially, the mechanical servo actuator hardware, feedback linkage, and surface position sensor remain fully functional ($\delta_{e,\text{plant}} = \delta_{e,\text{comp}}$ subject to first-order actuator lag $\tau_e = 0.0495\text{ s}$, bandwidth $\omega_{bw} = 20.2\text{ rad/s}$, and physical stops $\pm 25^\circ$, $\pm 60^\circ\text{/s}$). The onboard sensor measures the true physical surface position $\delta_{e,\text{meas}}(t) \approx \delta_{e,\text{comp}}(t)$. Consequently, $\delta_{e,\text{meas}} / \delta_{e,\text{cmd}} \approx 1.0$ at all times; no effectiveness information is directly present in the position telemetry. The sequence estimator is therefore forced to solve the genuine aerodynamic system-identification problem: inferring $\gamma_e$ by cross-correlating commanded and measured deflections against the aircraft's inertial and pitch-rate response ($q, \alpha, \dot{h}, V_c$).

In the out-of-scope propulsion boundary tests (Section \ref{sec:boundary}), propulsive degradation is modeled as a thrust-limiter ceiling fault:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{\text{th,plant}}(t) = \gamma_{\text{th}}(t) \, \delta_{\text{th,MIL}}, \qquad \delta_{\text{th,MIL}} = 0.50
    \label{eq:gamma_throttle}
\end{equation}
which caps available throttle command relative to the military power detent ($\delta_{\text{th}} = 0.50$).

\section{Baseline Flight Controller and Simulation Plant Verification Contracts}
\label{sec:baseline}

\subsection{Total Energy Control System (TECS) Baseline Formulation}
Nominal longitudinal trajectory regulation is governed by an energy-hold controller formulated using Lambregts' Total Energy Control System (TECS) principles \cite{Lambregts1983}. Specific total energy $\tilde{E}_T$ and specific energy distribution $\tilde{E}_D$ (in units of length) and their time derivatives are:
\begin{align}
    \tilde{E}_T &= h + \frac{V^2}{2g}, \qquad \tilde{E}_D = h - \frac{V^2}{2g} \\
    \dot{\tilde{E}}_T &= \dot{h} + \frac{V}{g}\dot{V}, \qquad \dot{\tilde{E}}_D = \dot{h} - \frac{V}{g}\dot{V}
\end{align}
Target energy rates are computed from reference altitude $h_{\text{ref}}$ and airspeed $V_{\text{ref}}$:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\tilde{E}}_{T,\text{cmd}} &= K_h (h_{\text{ref}} - h) + \frac{V_0}{g} K_V (V_{\text{ref}} - V) \\
    \dot{\tilde{E}}_{D,\text{cmd}} &= K_h (h_{\text{ref}} - h) - \frac{V_0}{g} K_V (V_{\text{ref}} - V)
\end{align}
Throttle command $\delta_{\text{th,cmd}} \in [0, 1]$ and baseline elevator command $\delta_{e,\text{base}}$ are synthesized via proportional-integral tracking with pitch-rate damping $K_q > 0$:
\begin{align}
    \delta_{\text{th,cmd}}(s) &= \left( K_{p,E} + \frac{K_{i,E}}{s} \right) \left[ \dot{\tilde{E}}_{T,\text{cmd}}(s) - \dot{\tilde{E}}_T(s) \right] \label{eq:tecs_th} \\
    \delta_{e,\text{base}}(s) &= \left( K_{p,B} + \frac{K_{i,B}}{s} \right) \left[ \dot{\tilde{E}}_{D,\text{cmd}}(s) - \dot{\tilde{E}}_D(s) \right] - K_q q(s) \label{eq:tecs_elev}
\end{align}

To prevent JSBSim's internal alpha limiter and roll-rate damping loops from filtering external actuator commands, Contract 2 enforces:
\begin{equation}
    \texttt{fcs/fbw-override} = 1.0, \quad \forall t \ge 0
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Contract 3: Envelope-Specific Trim and Propulsive Command Ownership}
Initial state vectors (\texttt{ic/h-sl-ft}, \texttt{ic/vc-kts}) are explicitly bound prior to calling native trim, ensuring unique equilibrium states across flight conditions: $10{,}000\text{ ft}/300\text{ kt}$ ($\theta_0 = 1.98^\circ$), $15{,}000\text{ ft}/400\text{ kt}$ ($\theta_0 = 0.490^\circ$), and $20{,}000\text{ ft}/450\text{ kt}$ ($\theta_0 = 0.11^\circ$). Propulsive response is verified by logging \texttt{propulsion/engine/n2} and net thrust.

\section{Integrity-Conditioned Reconfiguration and Analytical Stability Margins}
\label{sec:integrity}

\subsection{Six-Mode Controller Hierarchy}
The PHI-Ctrl architecture computes the compensated elevator command $\delta_{e,\text{comp}}(t) \in [-1, 1]$ via feedforward gain scaling $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$, inner-loop pitch-rate MRAC augmentation $u_{\text{mrac}}(t)$, and gated PPO residual $u_{\text{rl}}(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{e,\text{comp}}(t) = \operatorname{clip}_{[-1, 1]} \Big( \kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e(t)) \big[ u_{\text{base}}(t) + u_{\text{mrac}}(t) \big] + u_{\text{rl}}(t) \Big)
    \label{eq:reconfig_law}
\end{equation}
where the gain scaling function is bounded by the safety floor $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$:
\begin{equation}
    \kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e) = \frac{1}{\max(\hat{\gamma}_e, \, \gamma_{\text{floor}})}
    \label{eq:kappa}
\end{equation}
The six evaluated controller modes are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{BASELINE}: $u_{\text{mrac}} = 0$, $\kappa = 1.0$, $u_{\text{rl}} = 0$ (fixed-gain TECS baseline).
    \item \texttt{TWIN\_KAPPA}: $u_{\text{mrac}} = 0$, $\kappa = \kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$, $u_{\text{rl}} = 0$ (isolated gain scaling).
    \item \texttt{RESIDUAL\_ONLY}: $u_{\text{mrac}} = 0$, $\kappa = 1.0$, $u_{\text{rl}}$ active (isolated RL residual; gate held shut when healthy).
    \item \texttt{TECS\_MRAC}: $u_{\text{mrac}}$ active, $\kappa = 1.0$, $u_{\text{rl}} = 0$ (direct pitch-rate MRAC).
    \item \texttt{HYBRID}: $u_{\text{mrac}}$ active, $\kappa = \kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$, $u_{\text{rl}} = 0$ (analytical core: TECS + MRAC + $\kappa$).
    \item \texttt{FULL\_STACK}: $u_{\text{mrac}}$ active, $\kappa = \kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$, and $u_{\text{rl}}$ active (full framework).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analytical Stability Margin via the Small-Gain Theorem}
We now derive an analytical sufficient stability condition bounding the permissible real-time estimation error $\tilde{\gamma}_e = \hat{\gamma}_e - \gamma_e$ in the linearized short-period pitch-rate loop.

Let $G(s) = \bm{C}_{sp}(s\bm{I} - \bm{A}_{sp})^{-1} \bm{B}_{sp,e}$ denote the linearized transfer function from elevator deflection to pitch rate $q(s)$, incorporating actuator lag $\frac{20.2}{s + 20.2}$ and computational delay ($e^{-0.02s}$). Let $K(s)$ denote the nominal linear pitch-rate feedback controller. Under healthy effectiveness ($\gamma_e = 1.0$), the nominal loop transfer function and complementary sensitivity function are:
\begin{equation}
    L(s) = G(s) K(s), \qquad T(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}
\end{equation}
with $H_\infty$ peak complementary sensitivity $\|T\|_\infty = \sup_\omega |T(j\omega)| = \TinfNominal$ at $\omega_p = 11.24\text{ rad/s}$, gain crossover frequency $\omega_{gc} = 10.85\text{ rad/s}$, phase margin $\text{PM} = 45.92^\circ$, gain margin $\text{GM} = 4.182$ ($12.43\text{ dB}$), and disk margin $\alpha_{\text{disk}} = 0.7317$.

When true effectiveness degrades to $\gamma_e \in (0, 1]$ and the estimator outputs $\hat{\gamma}_e > 0$ with gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$ (below saturation), the perturbed open-loop transfer function is:
\begin{equation}
    L_\Delta(s) = G(s) \cdot \gamma_e \cdot \frac{1}{\hat{\gamma}_e} \cdot K(s) = L(s) \frac{\gamma_e}{\hat{\gamma}_e} = L(s) [1 + \Delta_m]
    \label{eq:loop_perturbed}
\end{equation}
where the static scalar multiplicative uncertainty $\Delta_m$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_m = \frac{\gamma_e}{\hat{\gamma}_e} - 1 = \frac{-\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e}, \qquad \tilde{\gamma}_e \triangleq \hat{\gamma}_e - \gamma_e
    \label{eq:delta_m}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Theorem 1 (Sufficient Estimation Error Bound via Small-Gain):} \textit{Suppose the nominal linearized closed-loop system $T(s) = L(s)(1+L(s))^{-1}$ is internally stable with $\|T\|_\infty > 1$, and suppose actuator limits and additive residual inputs are inactive. By the Small-Gain Theorem, a sufficient condition for asymptotic stability of the perturbed linearized closed-loop system under gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$ is:}
\begin{equation}
    |\Delta_m| < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty}
    \label{eq:small_gain_cond}
\end{equation}
\textit{which is satisfied if the estimation error $\tilde{\gamma}_e = \hat{\gamma}_e - \gamma_e$ lies within the asymmetric open interval:}
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{\gamma_e}{1 + \|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}
    \label{eq:stability_bound}
\end{equation}

\textit{Proof:} By the Small-Gain Theorem \cite{Lavretsky2013}, the feedback interconnection of stable $T(s)$ and static scalar perturbation $\Delta_m$ is internally asymptotically stable if $\|\Delta_m T(s)\|_\infty = |\Delta_m| \|T\|_\infty < 1$. Substituting (\ref{eq:delta_m}) yields:
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} < \frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e} < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty}
\end{equation}
Since $\hat{\gamma}_e = \gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e > 0$, multiplying through by $(\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e)$ preserves the inequalities:
\begin{align}
    -\frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} (\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e) < \tilde{\gamma}_e &\implies \tilde{\gamma}_e > -\frac{\gamma_e}{1 + \|T\|_\infty} \label{eq:lower_bound} \\
    \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} (\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e) &\implies \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1} \label{eq:upper_bound}
\end{align}
Combining (\ref{eq:lower_bound}) and (\ref{eq:upper_bound}) yields (\ref{eq:stability_bound}). $\blacksquare$

\textbf{Scope and Numerical Interpretation of Theorem 1:}
Theorem 1 provides an analytical \textit{sufficient} (not necessary-and-sufficient) stability condition for the linearized scalar short-period loop. Actuator rate/position limits and additive residual inputs $u_{\text{rl}}$ are excluded from the linear transfer function. For our linearized JSBSim plant ($\|T\|_\infty = \TinfNominal$) at $\gamma_e = 0.50$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Small-Gain Lower Bound:} $\tilde{\gamma}_e > -\frac{0.50}{1 + \TinfNominal} = \SmallGainBoundNominal \implies \hat{\gamma}_e > \SmallGainMinGammaHat$.
    \item \textbf{Disk Margin Bound:} $\tilde{\gamma}_e > \DiskMarginBoundNominal \implies \hat{\gamma}_e > 0.2887$.
    \item \textbf{Exact Linear Nyquist Bound:} $\tilde{\gamma}_e > \NyquistBoundNominal \implies \hat{\gamma}_e > \NyquistMinGammaHat$.
    \item \textbf{Upper Bound:} $\tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{0.50}{\TinfNominal - 1} = +1.761$ (physically capped at $+0.50$ since $\hat{\gamma}_e \le 1.0$).
\end{itemize}
\noindent\textbf{Remark on Dynamic Latency, Stability, and the Safety Floor:} 
Theorem 1 formulates estimation error as an algebraic perturbation on the feedback gain under quasi-static assumptions. In closed-loop operation, the sequence estimator introduces a finite sensing and inference latency ($\tau_d \approx 0.28\text{ s}$). During this transient delay window, the inner loop temporarily operates at the nominal pre-fault feedback gain ($\kappa = 1.0$), which corresponds to uncompensated surface degradation ($\Delta_m = \gamma_e - 1.0$). At the nominal design point ($\gamma_e = 0.50$), this induces a transient perturbation $|\Delta_m| = 0.50$, which lies safely within the Small-Gain robustness disk radius ($1/\|T\|_\infty = 1/1.2840 = 0.7788$). Because the open-loop short-period mode is stable ($\lambda_{1,2} = -0.8986 \pm 0.4493j\text{ rad/s}$), the plant does not undergo open-loop divergent excursion during this $0.28\text{ s}$ sensing window.

However, consider severe structural damage at $\gamma_e = 0.25$: prior to estimator convergence, the uncompensated perturbation is $|\Delta_m| = |0.25 - 1.0| = 0.75$, which closely approaches the Small-Gain robustness boundary ($0.7788$). If feedback gains were aggressively scaled below $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ ($\kappa > 2.857$), any transient estimation undershoot would immediately drive the perturbed loop gain outside the Small-Gain disk, precipitating closed-loop instability or actuator rate saturation. Enforcing $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ guarantees that the closed-loop system maintains a deterministic buffer above the theoretical stability limit ($\SmallGainMinGammaHat$).

\subsection{Integrity-Gated PPO Residual Policy}
The additive residual policy $u_{\text{rl}}(t)$ provides nonlinear trim augmentation under severe damage. The policy is parameterized as a two-layer MLP ($64 \times 64$, $\tanh$ activations) trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) \cite{Schulman2017} with observation vector:
\begin{equation}
    \bm{o}_t = \big[ e_h, \, e_\theta, \, q, \, q_m, \, \hat{\gamma}_e \big]^T
\end{equation}
PPO training hyperparameters are: actor/critic learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$, discount $\gamma_{\text{rl}} = 0.99$, GAE $\lambda = 0.95$, clipping parameter $\epsilon = 0.20$, batch size 64, entropy coefficient $0.01$, and 500 training episodes with randomized fault onset $t_{\text{fault}} \in [8, 15]\text{ s}$ and $\gamma_e \in [0.30, 0.85]$.

To enforce deterministic containment, the residual output is gated by a dual-condition hysteresis trigger:
\begin{equation}
    g(t) = \begin{cases}
        1, & \text{if } \hat{\gamma}_e(t) < 0.85 \text{ and } |q(t) - q_m(t)| > 0.20\text{ deg/s} \\
        0, & \text{if } \hat{\gamma}_e(t) > 0.90
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
clamped to a hard magnitude authority limit $|u_{\text{rl}}(t)| \le 2.0^\circ$ ($0.08$ normalized), and rate-limited to $|\dot{u}_{\text{rl}}| \le 20.0^\circ\text{/s}$ ($0.40\text{/step}$ at $50\text{ Hz}$).

\section{Multivariate Sequence Estimation and Conformal Certification Layer}
\label{sec:twin}

\subsection{Network Architecture}
The PHI-Twin sequence estimator processes an 8-channel multivariate telemetry window $W_k \in \mathbb{R}^{50 \times 8}$ ($1.0\text{ s}$ of history at $50\text{ Hz}$):
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}_t = \big[ q, \, \alpha, \, V_c, \, \theta, \, \dot{h}, \, \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}, \, \delta_{e,\text{meas}}, \, \delta_{\text{th,cmd}} \big]^T
    \label{eq:feat_vec}
\end{equation}
where $\delta_{e,\text{meas}}$ is the physical surface position measured by the onboard actuator position sensor (incorporating first-order actuator lag $\tau_e = 0.0495\text{ s}$ and position limits $\pm 25^\circ$) with zero-mean sensor noise ($\sigma = 0.003$). As established in Section \ref{sec:dynamics}, $\delta_{e,\text{meas}}$ reflects mechanical servo deflection rather than aerodynamic force, preventing trivial direct ratio leakage ($\delta_{e,\text{plant}}/\delta_{e,\text{cmd}} \approx 1.0$). The sequence estimator is therefore forced to solve the genuine aerodynamic system-identification problem: inferring control effectiveness degradation $\gamma_e$ by cross-correlating commanded and measured surface deflections against the resulting inertial and kinematic response ($q, \alpha, \dot{h}, V_c$).
Features are normalized by offline dataset running statistics. The network comprises:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Three parallel 1D convolutional layers with kernel sizes $k \in \{3, 5, 7\}$ (64 filters each, LeakyReLU $\alpha = 0.1$, BatchNorm), extracting multi-scale temporal derivatives and phase delays.
    \item A two-layer Bidirectional LSTM (128 units per direction, 256 hidden features total) capturing bidirectional causal relationships.
    \item A fully connected regression head ($256 \to 64 \to 1$) with Sigmoid activation mapping to $\hat{\gamma}_e \in [0.25, 1.0]$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Split-Conformal Certification Layer}
\label{sec:conformal}
While empirical point accuracy is necessary, airworthiness certification demands rigorous statistical confidence intervals bounding real-time estimation error in units of the instability boundary. We introduce a one-sided split-conformal prediction framework \cite{Vovk2005,Tibshirani2019} designed to certify lower estimation bounds.

Because feedforward gain inflation occurs specifically when effectiveness is underestimated ($\hat{\gamma}_e < \gamma_e$, or $\tilde{\gamma}_e < 0$), we formulate the one-sided nonconformity score as:
\begin{equation}
    s_i = \gamma_{e,i} - \hat{\gamma}_{e,i} = -\tilde{\gamma}_{e,i}
    \label{eq:nonconformity_score}
\end{equation}
where positive values of $s_i$ denote dangerous underestimation. The $n = 1600$ held-out window dataset is split into a stratified calibration set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ ($n_{\text{cal}} = 800$) and an independent evaluation set $\mathcal{D}_{\text{test}}$ ($n_{\text{test}} = 800$), stratified across all 14 damage levels.

For a target miscoverage rate $\alpha = 0.01$ (guaranteeing $\ge 99\%$ finite-sample coverage), the conformal quantile $\hat{q}_{\alpha}$ on $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}$ is computed as:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{q}_{\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left(\{s_i\}_{i \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}}}, \, \frac{\lceil (n_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{n_{\text{cal}}}\right)
    \label{eq:conformal_quantile}
\end{equation}
By the split-conformal validity theorem \cite{Vovk2005}, for any exchangeable future observation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{P}\left(\gamma_{e,n+1} - \hat{\gamma}_{e,n+1} \le \hat{q}_{\alpha}\right) \ge 1 - \alpha \iff \mathbb{P}\left(\tilde{\gamma}_{e,n+1} \ge -\hat{q}_{\alpha}\right) \ge 1 - \alpha
\end{equation}
Evaluating Eq. (\ref{eq:conformal_quantile}) at $\alpha = 0.01$ yields the global conformal quantile $\hat{q}_{0.01} = \ConformalQuantile$. Therefore, with $\ge 99\%$ statistical confidence, the real-time estimation error satisfies $\tilde{\gamma}_e \ge -\ConformalQuantile$.

\subsection{Design-Point Flight Condition and Evaluation Metrics}
The primary evaluation flight condition is level cruise trim at $h_0 = 15{,}000\text{ ft}$, $V_{\text{TAS},0} = 392.1\text{ kt}$ ($V_{c,0} = 317.3\text{ kt}$, $\text{Mach } 0.6258$, $\bar{q} = 15.68\text{ kPa}$, $\theta_0 = 1.66^\circ$, $\delta_{\text{th},0} = 0.2819$). Trajectory tracking is commanded as a $+500\text{ ft}$ altitude step prefiltered by a 2nd-order reference filter ($\omega_n = 0.30\text{ rad/s}, \zeta = 0.90$) at $t = 5.0\text{ s}$. Control-surface degradation $\gamma_e \in \{1.00, 0.80, 0.60, 0.50\}$ is injected at $t = 10.0\text{ s}$. 

\textbf{Metric Validity Remark on Peak Altitude Error:} 
In classical flight-control literature, peak tracking error $e_{h,\max} = \max_t |h(t) - h_{\text{ref}}(t)|$ is frequently cited as a primary figure of merit. In this experimental setup, however, $e_{h,\max}$ is structurally uninformative regarding fault tolerance. Because the climb command initiates at $t = 5.0\text{ s}$ while fault onset occurs at $t = 10.0\text{ s}$, the peak tracking error ($e_{h,\max} \approx 46.10\text{ ft}$) is established at $t \approx 9.8\text{ s}$ during the initial reference prefilter ramp prior to fault injection. Consequently, $e_{h,\max}$ is nearly identical across all fault severities ($\Delta e_{h,\max} = -0.0768\text{ ft}$), and its operational significance on peak excursion alone is practically negligible. The true physical metric of reconfigurable fault compensation is cumulative tracking energy $\text{ISE}_h$ and transient pitch-rate damping over the post-fault recovery window ($t \in [10, 60]\text{ s}$).

Tracking performance is quantified by:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Peak Tracking Error:} $e_{h,\max} = \max_{t \ge 10.0} |h(t) - h_{\text{ref}}(t)|$ [ft].
    \item \textbf{Cumulative Tracking Energy:} $\text{ISE}_h = \int_{10.0}^{60.0} [h(t) - h_{\text{ref}}(t)]^2 \, dt$ [$\text{ft}^2\text{s}$].
    \item \textbf{Actuator Variation:} $\text{TV}(\delta_e) = \sum_{k} |\delta_{e,\text{comp}}(t_k) - \delta_{e,\text{comp}}(t_{k-1})|$.
    \item \textbf{Pitch Rate Total Variation:} $\text{TV}_q = \sum_{k} |q(t_k) - q(t_{k-1})|$ [deg].
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Severe Damage Regime Where the Safety Floor Binds (WP-4)}

Enforcing $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ empirically demarcates the boundary between bounded, saturation-limited linear reconfiguration and physical actuator exhaustion, below which remaining authority must be allocated via reachable-set flight envelope protection rather than gain scaling.

\subsection{Mechanical Jam / Lock Fault Probe (WP-6)}
To explore structural limits, we injected an elevator lock fault at $t = 10.0\text{ s}$ ($\delta_e \equiv 0.0^\circ$ fixed). Because physical deflection ceases, position-to-command ratio collapses. The CNN-BiLSTM detects the severe authority loss and drives $\hat{\gamma}_e \to 0.299$ (clamping at the safety floor). However, because a multiplicative gain scaler $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e) \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}$ cannot actuate a mechanically frozen surface, cumulative tracking energy diverges ($\text{ISE}_h \approx 3.8 \times 10^6\text{ ft}^2\text{s}$). This highlights a fundamental scope boundary: multiplicative effectiveness estimation addresses surface area reduction or hydraulic pressure degradation, whereas additive mechanical jams require secondary effector allocation (e.g., differential stabilator or thrust vectoring), which remains outside the scope of single-surface reconfiguration.

\section{Discussion and Airworthiness Considerations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Authority Limits vs. Control Reconfiguration}
The experimental results demonstrate that while adaptive gain scaling $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ restores linear loop gain under aerodynamic surface impairment, it cannot overcome hard physical saturation limits. Under $\gamma_e = 0.50$, the maximum available pitching moment is halved ($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = \bar{q} S \bar{c} |C_{m_{\delta_e}}| \gamma_e \delta_{e,\max}$). At $392.1\text{ kt}$ TAS ($\bar{q} = 15.68\text{ kPa}$), remaining surface control power is ample to execute $500\text{ ft}$ climb transitions without exceeding the $\pm 25^\circ$ stabilator limit. However, attempting to scale gains below $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ rapidly drives actuator commands into position saturation, transforming an adaptive control problem into a constrained reachable-set problem.

\subsection{Architectural Safety Safeguards and Airworthiness Considerations}
While formal certification of learning-augmented flight software requires comprehensive verification under DO-178C \cite{DO178C}, DO-254 \cite{DO254}, and emerging AI assurance frameworks (e.g., EASA Level 2 guidance \cite{EASA2021}), the PHI-Ctrl architecture incorporates deterministic, defensible safeguards designed to facilitate airworthiness compliance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Baseline:} Trajectory guidance is anchored by the classical TECS baseline and plant verification contracts.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Gain Clamping:} Gain scaling $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ is constrained by the Small-Gain stability interval of Theorem 1 and clamped at $\kappa_{\max} = 2.85$ ($\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$).
    \item \textbf{Bounded Residual Authority:} The PPO residual policy is magnitude-clipped ($|u_{\text{rl}}| \le 2.0^\circ$), rate-limited ($20.0^\circ\text{/s}$), and gated off during healthy flight.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented the Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl) framework for certifying learned control-effectiveness estimators against analytical stability bounds on a nonlinear 6-DOF F-16 aircraft. The primary conclusions aligned with our core contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Small-Gain Stability Margin (C1):} We derived a closed-form sufficient stability condition bounding real-time effectiveness estimation error $\tilde{\gamma}_e$ in the linearized short-period loop ($-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$). This established mathematically that underestimation is fundamentally more hazardous than overestimation, motivating an adaptive gain safety floor of $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$.
    
    \item \textbf{Conformal Estimator Certification (C2):} A split-conformal verification layer on $n = 1600$ held-out test windows established a finite-sample distribution-free bound ($q_{\alpha=0.01} = 0.0477$), certifying that worst-case underestimation preserves a $4.59\times$ safety margin to the Small-Gain instability boundary at the $\gamma_e = 0.50$ design point and $3.21\times$ at the $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ safety floor, with empirical coverage $\ge 99.0\%$.
    
    \item \textbf{Cross-Channel Decoupling vs.\ Misattribution (C3):} Under an unmodeled throttle ceiling fault, 8-channel sequence estimation decoupled propulsion loss from elevator health ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = 457.5^\circ$). In contrast, architecturally matched 1-channel and 3-channel comparators misattributed deceleration as elevator damage, driving pitch variation to $\text{TV}_q = 880.7^\circ$ ($+92.5\%$), confirming that input-channel observability dictates fault isolation.
    
    \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Detector Calibration Contrast (C4):} Closed-loop audits demonstrated that trim excitation starvation causes classical MMAE banks and GainRatio detectors to suffer calibration collapse ($100\%$ false alarms, MAE $0.48$), whereas deep sequence estimation retains calibration ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$, $0\%$ false alarms).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Plant Implementation Lessons and Simulation Verification Contracts}
\label{sec:appendix}

Auditing the JSBSim F-16 simulation plant uncovered five implementation lessons essential for reproducible flight-control research:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Turbine Engine Re-Initialization Bug:} In JSBSim's \texttt{FGTurbine} class, calling \texttt{propulsion/set-running = -1} inside the per-step loop repeatedly invokes \texttt{InitRunning()}, resetting core speed to $N_2 = 99.34\%$ and net thrust to $16{,}160\text{ lbf}$, causing an uncommanded $\approx 571\text{ ft}$ climb drift. Setting this property once prior to trim and mapping throttle as \texttt{fcs/throttle-pos-norm = 2.0 * thr\_cmd} ensures proper dry and afterburner authority.
    \item \textbf{Simulation Timestep Synchronization ($100\text{ Hz}$ vs. $50\text{ Hz}$):} The JSBSim physics integration runs at $100\text{ Hz}$ ($\Delta t = 10\text{ ms}$), synchronized at an exact 2:1 integer sub-stepping ratio with the $50\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ ms}$) digital flight control loop, eliminating fractional sub-stepping clock drift.
    \item \textbf{FBW Override Enforcement:} Setting \texttt{fcs/elevator-cmd-norm} does not bypass the F-16 internal pitch damper unless \texttt{fcs/fbw-override = 1.0} is explicitly asserted on every step.
    \item \textbf{Standard Atmosphere Conditions:} At $h = 15{,}000\text{ ft}$, standard atmospheric density is $\rho = 0.001496\text{ slug/ft}^3$ ($0.7708\text{ kg/m}^3$) and speed of sound is $a = 626.5\text{ kt}$ ($322.3\text{ m/s}$). At cruise trim ($V_{\text{TAS}} = 392.1\text{ kt} = 661.7\text{ ft/s}$), this corresponds to calibrated airspeed $V_c = 317.3\text{ kt}$, $\text{Mach } 0.6258$, and dynamic pressure $\bar{q} = 15.68\text{ kPa}$ ($327.6\text{ psf}$). In supporting envelopes, $400\text{ kt TAS}$ corresponds to $\bar{q} = 18.63\text{ kPa}$ ($V_c = 343.7\text{ kt}$) at $10{,}000\text{ ft}$, and $\bar{q} = 13.13\text{ kPa}$ ($V_c = 292.0\text{ kt}$) at $20{,}000\text{ ft}$.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Seed Control:} Independent NumPy generators (\texttt{default\_rng(seed)}) are initialized per episode to ensure reproducible Dryden gusts and sensor noise.
\end{enumerate}
