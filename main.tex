\begin{abstract}
When an F-16 loses elevator authority, an erroneous control-effectiveness estimate in the adaptive loop can destabilize the vehicle faster than passive, unadapted flight. This paper presents the Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl) framework, formulating the reconfigurable control problem through the lens of analytical estimator certification. Using the Small-Gain Theorem, we establish an analytical sufficient stability condition bounding real-time estimation error in the linearized short-period loop ($-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$), defining an asymmetric design-point lower bound of $\tilde{\gamma}_e > \SmallGainBoundNominal$ ($\hat{\gamma}_e > \SmallGainMinGammaHat$) at $50\%$ elevator loss ($\gamma_e = 0.50$) and motivating an adaptive safety floor of $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = \SafetyFloorVal$ ($\kappa_{\max} = 2.857$). Across $n = 1600$ held-out test windows, a split-conformal verification layer establishes a distribution-free marginal coverage guarantee $\ge \ConformalCoverage$ ($99.0\%$ empirical test coverage, $q_{\alpha=0.01} = \ConformalQuantile$), certifying that worst-case underestimation preserves a $\ConformalMarginDesignPoint$ safety margin to the Small-Gain boundary at the design point ($\ConformalMarginSafetyFloor$ at the safety floor, with empirical sample margins $>3.7\times$ globally, distinguishing the distribution-free conformal bound margin of $2.75\times$ at $\gamma_e = 0.30$ from observed sample minima). Crucially, an out-of-scope propulsion boundary test demonstrates that 8-channel sequence estimation isolates unmodeled throttle ceiling faults ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = \TvQFullStackThrottle$), whereas single-channel adaptation misattributes airspeed decay as elevator loss ($\hat{\gamma}_e \to 0.32$), driving feedforward gain to maximum and triggering a $\TvQThrottleSpikePct$ pitch variation penalty ($\text{TV}_q = \TvQMisattrThrottle$ vs. $\TvQFullStackThrottle$). Closed-loop audits confirm that classical MMAE and gain-ratio detectors suffer severe calibration collapse under low trim excitation ($100\%$ false alarms, MAE $0.48$), whereas the sequence estimator retains uniform accuracy ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$) with $0\%$ false alarms under matched fairness protocols. All software, pre-trained weights, and simulation telemetry are open-sourced under the MIT License at \url{https://github.com/Sm-bello/PHI-CTRL} and permanently archived on Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.22767079).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

When modern high-performance aircraft suffer severe in-flight effector degradation—such as partial stabilator separation, hydraulic pressure loss, or battle damage—the effective control authority of the impaired aerodynamic surface drops precipitously \cite{Nguyen2007, Ward2002}. In fly-by-wire flight control systems, adaptive reconfiguration schemes such as Model Reference Adaptive Control (MRAC), $\mathcal{L}_1$ adaptive control, and Incremental Nonlinear Dynamic Inversion (INDI) are deployed to restore handling qualities by increasing loop gain or compensating for lost control effectiveness \cite{Lavretsky2013, Hovakimyan2010, Bacon2001, Smeur2016}. 

However, closing an adaptive feedforward or feedback loop on an online estimate of control effectiveness $\hat{\gamma}_e$ creates a critical, under-addressed safety vulnerability. If the estimator underestimates remaining effectiveness ($\hat{\gamma}_e < \gamma_e$), the adaptive scaling gain $\kappa = 1/\max(\hat{\gamma}_e, \gamma_{\text{floor}})$ over-amplifies control deflections, driving open-loop short-period dynamics across the closed-loop instability boundary. In turbulent atmospheric conditions or during large maneuvers, an uncertified estimator can destabilize the vehicle significantly faster than passive, unadapted flight. Conversely, classical fault detection and isolation (FDI) heuristics such as Multiple Model Adaptive Estimation (MMAE) or surface gain-ratio monitors often suffer calibration collapse during quiescent trim flight, where lack of persistent excitation starves the estimator of diagnostic information \cite{Maybeck1999, Cobleigh1997, Bosworth1992}.

To resolve this challenge, this paper presents the \textbf{Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl)} framework, evaluated on a nonlinear 6-DOF JSBSim F-16 aircraft model. This study is framed not as a novel control-law contribution, but as an \textbf{estimator certification methodology}, using feedforward gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\max(\hat{\gamma}_e, \gamma_{\text{floor}})$ as the baseline law that makes the analytical stability question well-posed. The core contributions of this study are organized around four distinct, verifiable claims:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Sufficient Stability Condition for Estimation Error (C1):} We derive a closed-form sufficient stability condition via the Small-Gain Theorem for multiplicative elevator effectiveness estimation errors $\tilde{\gamma}_e$ in the linearized scalar short-period pitch-rate loop. We prove that asymptotic stability of the linearized loop is guaranteed if $-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$, explaining mathematically why underestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e < 0$) is significantly more hazardous than overestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e > 0$), and providing an analytical derivation for an adaptive gain safety floor $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ ($\kappa_{\max} = 2.857$).
    
    \item \textbf{Conformal Estimator Certification Methodology (C2):} We introduce a distribution-free split-conformal certification layer on an 8-channel multivariate CNN-BiLSTM sequence estimator (PHI-Twin). Across $n = 1600$ held-out test windows evaluated at $\alpha = 0.01$ ($\ge 99\%$ confidence), the conformal underestimation bound $q_{\alpha=0.01} = 0.0477$ certifies a $4.59\times$ safety margin to the Small-Gain instability boundary at the $\gamma_e = 0.50$ design point and $3.21\times$ at the $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ safety floor, with empirical test coverage $\ge 99.0\%$ globally.
    
    \item \textbf{Input-Channel Decoupling vs.\ Out-of-Scope Fault Misattribution (C3):} By retraining architecturally identical 1-channel ($[q]$), 3-channel ($[q, \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}, \delta_{e,\text{meas}}]$), and 8-channel CNN-BiLSTMs under a matched fairness protocol, we demonstrate that input-channel observability—not model capacity—governs cross-channel fault isolation. When exposed to an unmodeled throttle ceiling fault ($\delta_{\text{th,plant}} = \gamma_{\text{th}} \times 0.50$), 8-channel sequence estimation isolates the fault ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = 19.15^\circ$), whereas single-channel adaptation misattributes airspeed decay as elevator loss ($\hat{\gamma}_e \to 0.322$), driving feedforward gain to maximum and triggering a $+228.8\%$ pitch variation penalty ($\text{TV}_q = 62.96^\circ$ vs. $19.15^\circ$).
    
    \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Detector Calibration Contrast (C4):} We execute a detector calibration audit comparing classical lightweight FDI schemes (MMAE hypothesis banks, rolling GainRatio, RLS) against sequence estimation across 160 closed-loop telemetry episodes subjected to pitch-rate gyro bias $b_q \in [0.0, 5.0]\text{ deg/s}$. We show that feedback-induced trim excitation starvation ($\sigma(\delta_{e,\text{cmd}}) \approx 0.008$) causes discrete MMAE posteriors to collapse ($100\%$ healthy false alarms) and GainRatio to suffer bias swamping ($\text{MAE} \approx 0.48$), whereas sequence estimation retains calibration ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$, $0\%$ false alarms).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Nonlinear Flight Dynamics and State-Space Formulation}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{Six-Degree-of-Freedom Equations of Motion}
In body-fixed coordinates $F_b = \{O_b, X_b, Y_b, Z_b\}$ centered at the aircraft center of mass, the rigid-body six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) translational equations of motion \cite{StevensLewis2003} are:
\begin{align}
    \dot{u} &= rv - qw - g \sin\theta + \frac{X_{\text{aero}} + T \cos\phi_T}{m} \label{eq:u_dot} \\
    \dot{v} &= pw - ru + g \cos\theta \sin\phi + \frac{Y_{\text{aero}}}{m} \label{eq:v_dot} \\
    \dot{w} &= qu - pv + g \cos\theta \cos\phi + \frac{Z_{\text{aero}} - T \sin\phi_T}{m} \label{eq:w_dot}
\end{align}
where $[u, v, w]^T$ is the body-axis velocity vector, $m$ is aircraft mass, $g$ is gravitational acceleration, $[\phi, \theta, \psi]^T$ are Euler roll, pitch, and yaw angles, $T$ is net propulsive thrust at cant angle $\phi_T$, and $[X_{\text{aero}}, Y_{\text{aero}}, Z_{\text{aero}}]^T$ are aerodynamic body forces.

Assuming lateral-directional mass symmetry ($I_{xy} = I_{yz} = 0$), the rotational equations of motion for body angular rates $[p, q, r]^T$ with inertia tensor $\bm{I}$ are:
\begin{align}
    \dot{p} &= \frac{I_{zz} \mathcal{L} + I_{xz} \mathcal{N} - \{I_{xz}(I_{yy} - I_{xx} - I_{zz})p + [I_{zz}(I_{zz} - I_{yy}) + I_{xz}^2]r\}q}{I_{xx} I_{zz} - I_{xz}^2} \label{eq:p_dot} \\
    \dot{q} &= \frac{\mathcal{M} + (I_{zz} - I_{xx})pr - I_{xz}(p^2 - r^2)}{I_{yy}} \label{eq:q_dot} \\
    \dot{r} &= \frac{I_{xz} \mathcal{L} + I_{xx} \mathcal{N} + \{I_{xz}(I_{yy} - I_{xx} - I_{zz})r + [I_{xx}(I_{xx} - I_{yy}) + I_{xz}^2]p\}q}{I_{xx} I_{zz} - I_{xz}^2} \label{eq:r_dot}
\end{align}
where $[\mathcal{L}, \mathcal{M}, \mathcal{N}]^T$ are total aerodynamic and propulsive rolling, pitching, and yawing moments.

Kinematic propagation of Euler attitude angles follows standard Poisson transformations:
\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix} \dot{\phi} \\ \dot{\theta} \\ \dot{\psi} \end{bmatrix} = 
\begin{bmatrix} 
1 & \sin\phi \tan\theta & \cos\phi \tan\theta \\
0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\
0 & \sin\phi \sec\theta & \cos\phi \sec\theta
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} p \\ q \\ r \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Aerodynamic Expansions and Short-Period Linearization}
Aerodynamic body forces and pitching moments are parameterized in stability axes via dynamic pressure $\bar{q} = \frac{1}{2}\rho V_T^2$, wing reference area $S$, and mean aerodynamic chord $\bar{c}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M} = \bar{q} S \bar{c} \left[ C_{m_0}(\alpha) + C_{m_\alpha} \alpha + C_{m_q} \frac{q\bar{c}}{2V_T} + C_{m_{\delta_e}} \delta_e + C_{m_{\delta_{\text{th}}}} \delta_{\text{th}} \right]
\end{equation}
Linearizing the longitudinal dynamics about trimmed wings-level equilibrium ($V_0 = 392.1\text{ kt TAS}$, $h_0 = 15{,}000\text{ ft}$, $\theta_0 = 0.490^\circ$, $\alpha_0 = 1.659^\circ$) yields the 2-state short-period state-space representation for state vector $\bm{x}_{\text{sp}} = [\Delta\alpha, \Delta q]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\bm{x}}_{\text{sp}} = \bm{A}_{\text{sp}} \bm{x}_{\text{sp}} + \bm{B}_{\text{sp}} \Delta\delta_e
\end{equation}
with numerical system matrices:
\begin{equation}
    \bm{A}_{\text{sp}} = \begin{bmatrix} Z_\alpha / V_0 & 1 + Z_q / V_0 \\ M_\alpha & M_q \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.8385 & 0.9237 \\ -0.2225 & -0.9586 \end{bmatrix}, \quad 
    \bm{B}_{\text{sp}} = \begin{bmatrix} Z_{\delta_e} / V_0 \\ M_{\delta_e} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} -0.8112 \\ -3.5577 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The open-loop short-period eigenvalues are $\lambda_{1,2} = -0.8986 \pm 0.4679j$, corresponding to natural frequency $\omega_{n,\text{sp}} = 1.013\text{ rad/s}$ and damping ratio $\zeta_{\text{sp}} = 0.887$, satisfying MIL-STD-1797A Category A flight phase requirements.

\subsection{Effector Degradation Models}
Actuator fault modeling represents continuous in-flight surface impairment as a multiplicative effectiveness loss:
\begin{equation}
    \delta_{e,\text{plant}}(t) = \gamma_e(t) \cdot \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}(t), \quad \gamma_e(t) \in [0.25, 1.00]
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_e = 1.0$ represents nominal effector authority, $\gamma_e = 0.50$ represents $50\%$ effectiveness loss (primary design point), and $\gamma_e < 0.35$ represents severe structural degradation where physical actuator saturation bounds begin to dominate linear reconfiguration. Actuator dynamics are modeled as a rate-limited first-order lag with bandwidth $\omega_{\text{act}} = 20.0\text{ rad/s}$, position limits $\delta_e \in [-25^\circ, +25^\circ]$, and slew rate limit $|\dot{\delta}_e| \le 60^\circ/\text{s}$.

\section{Baseline Flight Controller and Simulation Plant Verification Contracts}
\label{sec:baseline}

\subsection{Total Energy Control System (TECS) Baseline Formulation}
The outer guidance loop employs a Total Energy Control System (TECS) \cite{Lambregts1983}. Specific total energy $E_s$ and specific energy distribution $D_s$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    E_s = h + \frac{V_T^2}{2g}, \quad D_s = h - \frac{V_T^2}{2g}
\end{equation}
Differentiating with respect to time yields total energy rate $\dot{E}_s$ and distribution rate $\dot{D}_s$:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{E}_s = \dot{h} + \frac{V_T \dot{V}_T}{g}, \quad \dot{D}_s = \dot{h} - \frac{V_T \dot{V}_T}{g}
\end{equation}
Total thrust command $\delta_{\text{th,cmd}}$ governs total energy rate, while symmetrical stabilator deflection $\delta_{e,\text{base}}$ commands energy balance between potential and kinetic energy:
\begin{align}
    \delta_{\text{th,cmd}} &= K_{P,E} (\dot{E}_{s,\text{ref}} - \dot{E}_s) + K_{I,E} (E_{s,\text{ref}} - E_s) \\
    \theta_{\text{cmd}} &= K_{P,D} (\dot{D}_{s,\text{ref}} - \dot{D}_s) + K_{I,D} (D_{s,\text{ref}} - D_s)
\end{align}
The inner loop converts $\theta_{\text{cmd}}$ into pitch-rate demands $q_{\text{cmd}} = K_\theta (\theta_{\text{cmd}} - \theta)$, driving baseline elevator command $\delta_{e,\text{base}}$.

\section{Integrity-Conditioned Reconfiguration and Analytical Stability Margins}
\label{sec:integrity}

\subsection{Six-Mode Controller Hierarchy}
To isolate the quantitative contribution of each algorithmic component, we define a formal six-mode controller hierarchy:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \texttt{BASELINE}: Pure TECS guidance loop with linear PID inner loop ($\kappa \equiv 1.0$).
    \item \texttt{TECS\_MRAC}: TECS outer loop augmented by classical direct pitch-rate MRAC.
    \item \texttt{TWIN\_KAPPA}: TECS augmented by adaptive feedforward gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\max(\hat{\gamma}_e, \gamma_{\text{floor}})$.
    \item \texttt{RESIDUAL\_ONLY}: TECS augmented by an un-gated PPO reinforcement learning residual.
    \item \texttt{HYBRID}: TECS + MRAC + $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ feedforward gain scheduling.
    \item \texttt{FULL\_STACK}: Complete PHI-Ctrl architecture (TECS + MRAC + $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ + integrity-gated PPO residual $u_{\text{rl}}$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analytical Stability Margin via the Small-Gain Theorem}
\label{sec:small_gain}

We formulate the robust stability problem by modeling estimation error as a feedback perturbation on the nominal closed-loop short-period loop. Let $\hat{\gamma}_e$ denote the real-time estimate of true effectiveness $\gamma_e$, and define estimation error as:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\gamma}_e = \hat{\gamma}_e - \gamma_e
\end{equation}
Under feedforward gain scaling $\kappa = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$, the effective plant input gain is:
\begin{equation}
    k_{\text{eff}} = \gamma_e \kappa = \frac{\gamma_e}{\hat{\gamma}_e} = \frac{\gamma_e}{\gamma_e + \tilde{\gamma}_e} = \frac{1}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}
\end{equation}
Expressing $k_{\text{eff}}$ as a nominal unity gain plus a multiplicative perturbation $\Delta(s)$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta = k_{\text{eff}} - 1 = \frac{-\tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e} = -\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\hat{\gamma}_e}
\end{equation}
Let $T(s)$ denote the nominal complementary sensitivity function of the pitch-rate short-period loop:
\begin{equation}
    T(s) = \frac{L(s)}{1 + L(s)}, \quad L(s) = C_q(s) G_{q/\delta_e}(s)
\end{equation}
where $G_{q/\delta_e}(s) = \frac{M_{\delta_e} s + (Z_\alpha M_{\delta_e} - M_\alpha Z_{\delta_e})/V_0}{s^2 - (Z_\alpha/V_0 + M_q)s + (Z_\alpha M_q / V_0 - M_\alpha(1 + Z_q/V_0))}$.

\newtheorem{theorem}{Theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Small-Gain Estimator Error Stability Bound]
\label{thm:small_gain}
Assume the nominal closed-loop short-period loop is internally stable ($T(s) \in \mathcal{RH}_\infty$). By the Small-Gain Theorem \cite{ZhouDoyle1998}, the closed-loop system under adaptive feedforward scaling $\kappa = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$ remains asymptotically stable for all stable perturbations satisfying $\|\Delta\|_\infty < 1/\|T\|_\infty$. A sufficient condition for closed-loop stability is:
\begin{equation}
    -\frac{\gamma_e}{1 + \|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1} \quad (\text{for } \|T\|_\infty > 1)
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\noindent\textbf{Proof:} The Small-Gain condition requires $|\Delta| < 1/\|T\|_\infty$. Substituting $\Delta = -\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}$:
\begin{equation}
    \left| \frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e} \right| < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty}
\end{equation}
For negative estimation error ($\tilde{\gamma}_e < 0$, estimator underestimation):
\begin{equation}
    \frac{-\tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e} < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} \implies -\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\gamma_e} < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} \left(1 + \frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\gamma_e}\right) \implies \tilde{\gamma}_e > -\frac{\gamma_e}{1 + \|T\|_\infty}
\end{equation}
For positive estimation error ($\tilde{\gamma}_e > 0$, estimator overestimation):
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e}{1 + \tilde{\gamma}_e / \gamma_e} < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} \implies \frac{\tilde{\gamma}_e}{\gamma_e} \left(1 - \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty}\right) < \frac{1}{\|T\|_\infty} \implies \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}
\end{equation}
which establishes the theorem. $\blacksquare$

\noindent\textbf{Physical Interpretation of Asymmetry:} Theorem \ref{thm:small_gain} explains why estimator underestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e < 0$) is fundamentally more hazardous than overestimation ($\tilde{\gamma}_e > 0$). When the estimator underestimates health ($\hat{\gamma}_e < \gamma_e$), $\kappa = 1/\hat{\gamma}_e$ commands excessively high loop gains, eroding phase and gain margins and destabilizing the inner loop. Conversely, when the estimator overestimates health ($\hat{\gamma}_e > \gamma_e$), loop gain is under-scaled ($\kappa < 1/\gamma_e$), which degrades tracking performance but does not destabilize the vehicle.

At the nominal $15{,}000\text{ ft} / 392.1\text{ kt}$ TAS aerodynamic design point, numerical evaluation yields peak complementary sensitivity $\|T\|_\infty = 1.2840$ (Fig. \ref{fig:bode_nyquist}). Substituting into Theorem \ref{thm:small_gain} for $\gamma_e = 0.50$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    -0.2189 < \tilde{\gamma}_e < +1.7606 \implies \hat{\gamma}_e > 0.2811
\end{equation}
This analytical boundary directly motivates our choice of adaptive safety floor $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ ($\kappa_{\max} = 2.857$), which guarantees that $\hat{\gamma}_e$ remains strictly above the analytical instability threshold $0.2811$ across the entire operational flight envelope.

\subsection{Integrity-Gated PPO Residual Policy}
To damp high-frequency post-fault transients without corrupting steady-state trim or conflicting with the baseline TECS controller, the PPO residual policy $u_{\text{rl}}$ is passed through an innovation and integrity gate:
\begin{equation}
    u_{\text{rl,gated}}(t) = g_{\text{innov}}(t) \cdot g_{\text{integ}}(t) \cdot \text{clip}(u_{\text{rl}}(t), -2.0^\circ, +2.0^\circ)
\end{equation}
where $g_{\text{innov}}(t) = \sigma(|q_m(t) - q_{\text{ref}}(t)| - \epsilon_q)$ activates only during anomalous dynamic innovations, and $g_{\text{integ}}(t) = \mathbb{I}(\hat{\gamma}_e(t) \ge \gamma_{\text{floor}})$ disables residual actuation if estimator integrity is compromised.

\subsection{Network Architecture}
The PHI-Twin estimator processes an 8-channel temporal feature sequence $\bm{X}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{50 \times 8}$ spanning a $1.0\text{ s}$ window ($50\text{ Hz}$ control rate):
\begin{equation}
    \bm{x}(k) = \left[ q(k), \alpha(k), V_c(k), \theta(k), \dot{h}(k), \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}(k), \delta_{e,\text{meas}}(k), \delta_{\text{th}}(k) \right]^T

The architecture comprises two 1D convolutional layers ($48$ filters, kernel sizes $5$ and $3$) with LeakyReLU activations ($\alpha = 0.1$), feeding a bidirectional LSTM layer ($64$ hidden units per direction, output dimension $128$). A fully connected multilayer perceptron ($128 \to 64 \to 1$) maps the final hidden state through a Sigmoid activation, scaled to $\hat{\gamma}_e \in [0.25, 1.00]$. Total trainable parameters are $98{,}545$.

\subsection{Conformal Estimator Certification Layer}
\label{sec:conformal}

To transform empirical safety margins into rigorous, distribution-free statistical guarantees, we wrap the frozen CNN-BiLSTM in a split-conformal prediction framework \cite{Vovk2005, Tibshirani2019, Angelopoulos2023}. Because underestimation ($\hat{\gamma}_e < \gamma_e$) erodes stability margins while overestimation is benign, we define a one-sided non-conformity score targeting underestimation:
\begin{equation}
    s_i = \gamma_e^{(i)} - \hat{\gamma}_e^{(i)} = -\tilde{\gamma}_e^{(i)}
\end{equation}
Using a calibration set of $n_{\text{cal}} = 800$ held-out windows stratified across damage levels, gust realizations, and onset times, we compute the empirical quantile at significance level $\alpha = 0.01$ ($99\%$ confidence):
\begin{equation}
    q_{\alpha} = \text{Quantile}\left( \{s_i\}_{i=1}^{n_{\text{cal}}}, \frac{\lceil (n_{\text{cal}} + 1)(1 - \alpha) \rceil}{n_{\text{cal}}} \right)
\end{equation}
Evaluating across the calibration set yields $q_{0.01} = 0.047716$. Under split-conformal theory, for any fresh test window drawn from the same data-generating distribution, the true effectiveness satisfies:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbb{P}\left( \gamma_e - \hat{\gamma}_e \le q_{0.01} \right) \ge 1 - \alpha = 0.99 \implies \mathbb{P}\left( \tilde{\gamma}_e \ge -0.0477 \right) \ge 0.99
\end{equation}
This establishes a distribution-free bound certifying that with $\ge 99\%$ confidence, the estimator will not underestimate true authority by more than $0.0477$.

\subsection{Metric Validity: Why Peak Altitude Error is Structurally Uninformative}
Before presenting Monte Carlo performance metrics, we address a subtle aerodynamic reality regarding tracking error evaluation. In earlier revisions, reviewers observed that maximum altitude tracking error $\overline{e_{h,\max}}$ was essentially indistinguishable between baseline and fully reconfigured controllers ($\approx 46.3\text{ ft}$). 

Detailed time-history auditing reveals that this maximum error occurs at $t \approx 9.8\text{ s}$—during the initial $+500\text{ ft}$ climb transient commanded at $t = 5.0\text{ s}$, \textbf{prior to fault injection at $t = 10.0\text{ s}$}. Because the climb step is filtered through a standard second-order reference model ($\omega_n = 0.30\text{ rad/s}$, $\zeta = 0.90$), both controllers experience identical pre-fault kinematic tracking lag. Consequently, $\overline{e_{h,\max}}$ is structurally uninformative regarding fault tolerance. The true indicators of fault-conditioned performance are post-fault cumulative tracking energy ($\text{ISE}_h = \int_{10}^{60} [h(t) - h_{\text{ref}}(t)]^2 dt$) and pitch-rate total variation ($\text{TV}_q = \sum |q(k) - q(k-1)|$), which capture trajectory restoration precision and high-frequency control chatter.

\subsection{Mechanical Jam / Lock Fault Probe (WP-6)}
\label{sec:wp6_jam}

To explore structural limits, we injected an elevator lock fault at $t = 10.0\text{ s}$ ($\delta_e \equiv 0.0^\circ$ fixed). Because physical deflection ceases, position-to-command ratio collapses. The CNN-BiLSTM detects the severe authority loss and drives $\hat{\gamma}_e \to 0.299$ (clamping at the safety floor). However, because a multiplicative gain scaler $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e) \delta_{e,\text{cmd}}$ cannot actuate a mechanically frozen surface, cumulative tracking energy diverges ($\text{ISE}_h \approx 3.8 \times 10^6\text{ ft}^2\text{s}$). This highlights a fundamental scope boundary: multiplicative effectiveness estimation addresses surface area reduction or hydraulic pressure degradation, whereas additive mechanical jams require secondary effector allocation (e.g., differential stabilator or thrust vectoring), which remains outside the scope of single-surface reconfiguration.

\section{Discussion and Airworthiness Considerations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Physical Authority Limits vs. Control Reconfiguration}
The experimental results demonstrate that while adaptive gain scaling $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ restores linear loop gain under aerodynamic surface impairment, it cannot overcome hard physical saturation limits. Under $\gamma_e = 0.50$, the maximum available pitching moment is halved ($\mathcal{M}_{\max} = \bar{q} S \bar{c} |C_{m_{\delta_e}}| \gamma_e \delta_{e,\max}$). At $392.1\text{ kt}$ TAS ($\bar{q} = 15.68\text{ kPa}$), remaining surface control power is ample to execute $500\text{ ft}$ climb transitions without exceeding the $\pm 25^\circ$ stabilator limit. However, attempting to scale gains below $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ rapidly drives actuator commands into position saturation, transforming an adaptive control problem into a constrained reachable-set problem.

\subsection{Architectural Safety Safeguards and Airworthiness Considerations}
While formal certification of learning-augmented flight software requires comprehensive verification under DO-178C \cite{DO178C}, DO-254 \cite{DO254}, and emerging AI assurance frameworks (e.g., EASA Level 2 guidance \cite{EASA2021}), the PHI-Ctrl architecture incorporates deterministic, defensible safeguards designed to facilitate airworthiness compliance:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Baseline:} Trajectory guidance is anchored by the classical TECS baseline and plant verification contracts.
    \item \textbf{Analytical Gain Clamping:} Gain scaling $\kappa(\hat{\gamma}_e)$ is constrained by the Small-Gain stability interval of Theorem \ref{thm:small_gain} and clamped at $\kappa_{\max} = 2.857$ ($\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$).
    \item \textbf{Bounded Residual Authority:} The PPO residual policy is magnitude-clipped ($|u_{\text{rl}}| \le 2.0^\circ$), rate-limited ($20.0^\circ\text{/s}$), and gated off during healthy flight.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper presented the Physics-Hybrid Integrity Flight Control (PHI-Ctrl) framework for certifying learned control-effectiveness estimators against analytical stability bounds on a nonlinear 6-DOF F-16 aircraft. The primary conclusions aligned with our core contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Small-Gain Stability Margin (C1):} We derived a closed-form sufficient stability condition bounding real-time effectiveness estimation error $\tilde{\gamma}_e$ in the linearized short-period loop ($-\frac{\gamma_e}{1+\|T\|_\infty} < \tilde{\gamma}_e < \frac{\gamma_e}{\|T\|_\infty - 1}$). This established mathematically that underestimation is fundamentally more hazardous than overestimation, motivating an adaptive gain safety floor of $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ ($\kappa_{\max} = 2.857$).
    
    \item \textbf{Conformal Estimator Certification (C2):} A split-conformal verification layer on $n = 1600$ held-out test windows established a finite-sample distribution-free bound ($q_{\alpha=0.01} = 0.0477$), certifying that worst-case underestimation preserves a $4.59\times$ safety margin to the Small-Gain instability boundary at the $\gamma_e = 0.50$ design point and $3.21\times$ at the $\gamma_{\text{floor}} = 0.35$ safety floor, with empirical marginal coverage $\ge 99.0\%$.
    
    \item \textbf{Cross-Channel Decoupling vs.\ Misattribution (C3):} Under an unmodeled throttle ceiling fault, 8-channel sequence estimation decoupled propulsion loss from elevator health ($\hat{\gamma}_e \approx 0.96\text{--}1.00$, $\text{TV}_q = 19.15^\circ$). In contrast, architecturally matched 1-channel and 3-channel comparators misattributed deceleration as elevator damage ($\hat{\gamma}_e \to 0.322$), driving feedforward gain to maximum and triggering a $+228.8\%$ pitch variation penalty ($\text{TV}_q = 62.96^\circ$), confirming that input-channel observability dictates fault isolation.
    
    \item \textbf{Closed-Loop Detector Calibration Contrast (C4):} Closed-loop audits demonstrated that trim excitation starvation causes classical MMAE banks and GainRatio detectors to suffer calibration collapse ($100\%$ false alarms, MAE $0.48$), whereas deep sequence estimation retains calibration ($\text{MAE} \le 0.033$, $0\%$ false alarms).
\end{enumerate}
